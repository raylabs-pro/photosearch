What it does

Describe it, find it

Type a whole sentence, not just keywords. PhotoSearch understands the sentence — not only the nouns in it.

"kid in the red shirt blowing out candles"

Reads the words in your photos

Receipts, wifi cards, signs, menus, whiteboards — if there's text in the picture, you can search for it.

"wifi password"

Objects and colors, together

The two are understood as one idea, not matched separately — so a search for a color means the right thing.

"red car"

Any folder, not just Photos

Index design references, exports, an archive drive — anywhere on disk, indexed and searched the same way.

the "+" chip under search

Tells you when nothing matches

Most search would rather show you something than admit it found nothing. We measured "god" against a real library at 0.4097 and "dog" at 0.4235 — four hundredths apart, no threshold separates them. So instead of a threshold, it checks for actual evidence: no tag, no caption, no OCR word, no answer.

search "asdf" — you get nothing, not a guess

Private, and honest about it

The models live in the app

The AI that reads your photos — object detection, text recognition, image understanding — ships inside the app itself, about 140 MB. Nothing is downloaded from a server to search your library.

Not sandboxed-and-still-phones-home. Actually can't.

PhotoSearch ships with no network entitlement at all — not unused, absent. A sandboxed Mac app without network.client cannot open a socket; macOS enforces that at the kernel, not us at our discretion. This isn't a policy we're promising to keep. It's a permission we don't have.

The one exception, stated plainly

If a photo carries GPS coordinates, turning them into a place name ("Rome", "Hamburg") uses Apple's own system geocoder — coordinates only, never a photo, handled by macOS itself, not by us. Search still works fully offline; only that one place-name lookup needs a connection. We'd rather name the one exception than round up to "100% local."

Frequently asked

Will it run on my Mac?

You need macOS 14 Sonoma or later on a Mac with Apple silicon — an M1, M2, M3, M4 or later chip. Intel Macs are not supported: the models that read your photos run on the Neural Engine, which Intel Macs do not have.

Not sure which you have? Apple menu → About This Mac. If the Chip line says "Apple M…" you are fine; if it says "Processor … Intel" the app will not run.

It needs about 350 MB free — 140 MB for the app itself and room for the search index it builds from your library.

Why does PhotoSearch ask for photo library access?

Search only works on photos the app can see. The library is scanned on your Mac — the models that understand your photos ship inside the app, and nothing about your photos is uploaded anywhere.

How long does the first index take?

A 10,000-photo library becomes searchable in minutes on Apple silicon. Your newest photos are indexed first, so search is useful almost immediately; older photos follow in the background.

Can I search folders that aren't in my Photos library?

Yes — click the + chip under the search field and pick any folder (design references, exports, an archive drive). Folder photos are indexed the same way and get their own filter chip.

Search finds text inside photos?

Yes. Words in your pictures are searchable: receipts, wifi cards, signs, menus, whiteboards, QR codes and barcodes. Try typing a word you remember seeing.

Something looks wrong — what should I try?

Quit and reopen the app; indexing resumes where it left off. If a specific search misbehaves, email a description of the query and what you expected — that's genuinely useful feedback for tuning search.
